# Wizzzee 0.6.1

Wizzzee is a disk space analyzer for macOS in the shape of [WizTree](https://diskanalyzer.com/): a folder tree with sizes, a list of the biggest files, and a colored treemap you can click into. It scans a full startup disk — around 3.9 million files — in roughly 15 seconds.

**This release moves one thing.** 0.6.0 took the count of what is marked, and the buttons that remove it, out of a chip in the status bar and put them in a bar across the foot of the window. The bar was easy to see and in the wrong place: at the far end of the window from the boxes being ticked, and from everything else that acts on a selection.

## What is marked, beside the tabs

From the first mark, the bar now fills the rest of the row the tabs are in, at the top of the window and on every tab:

`Tree View  File View  About  ☑ 3 items marked for removal · 424 MB on disk · Show List ⌄ ……… Clear Marks · Move to Trash · Delete…`

- **It is where the eye already is.** The header's own buttons for a plain selection are just above it, and the column the boxes are ticked in is just below.
- **The first mark moves nothing.** The tabs' row is one height with the bar in it or not, so the box you just ticked stays under the pointer. Click it again without moving and the mark comes back off.
- **Show List drops the list down under the row**, where it used to open upwards from the foot. It is still a row for each mark, with its size, where it is, and a way to take the mark back off; still as tall as its rows up to ten; and still gives way and scrolls in a short window.
- **The open list keeps the height it opened with.** It is above the table now, and a list that grew with every box ticked would push the table down a row under the pointer. A mark made while it is open is one more row to scroll to; hide the list and show it again and it is as tall as what is marked by then.
- Nothing is at the foot of the window now but the status bar.

What the buttons do has not changed. **Move to Trash** moves everything marked without asking, and `⌘Z` brings it back; **Delete…** asks first.

The guide's page on marking is redrawn to match: its picture has the tabs and the bar at the top, and its second card is *The bar beside the tabs*. If you ticked **Don't show this again**, it is under **Help ▸ Welcome to Wizzzee**.

## Limits

- **`⌘Q` does nothing while the guide is open.** A window with a sheet over it does not quit, and the guide is a sheet. Close the guide first — `Esc` does it — and then quit. This is in 0.6.0 as well.
- **Taking the last mark off with the list open shuts the list**, and the table moves back up by the list's height. Nothing else that marks or unmarks moves it.
- **Closing the Full Disk Access banner while something is marked** moves the tabs' row, bar and all, up into the banner's place. A second click where the banner's ✕ was is then on the bar's **Delete…**, which asks, and names what would go, before it does anything.
- In a window at its shortest, with the treemap showing, the list of marks is about seven rows high and scrolls. It takes its full ten from a window about 730 points high.
- A plain multiple selection — several rows picked with Shift or `⌘`, none of them ticked — still has its two buttons at the top right, under **Size / On Disk**. They are now just above the bar for the marks, and are not the same thing: one pair acts on what is selected, the other on what is marked.
- The tabs' row is 11 points taller than it was, so the tables are 11 points shorter.

## Installing

Download `Wizzzee-0.6.1-macos-universal.zip` below, unzip it, and move `Wizzzee.app` to Applications, replacing the old one.

- **First launch.** The app is not notarized, so macOS blocks it the first time. Open it once, dismiss the warning, then go to **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and click **Open Anyway**. macOS asks only once.
- **Full Disk Access has to be granted again.** macOS ties that permission to the exact build, so the grant from an earlier version does not carry over. In **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access**, remove the old Wizzzee entry and add the new app. Without it, Mail, Messages, Safari and other users' folders are skipped and the totals read low.

It runs on macOS 14 or newer, on Apple silicon and Intel.

## Internal

The bar and the list are the same two views as in 0.6.0, in a different place: the bar inside the tabs' row, and the list between that row and the tab's contents. The one thing that needed care was the first mark. A strip added above a table pushes the table down by its own height, under a pointer that has just clicked in it. So the row was made one height for good, 42 points, and the bar goes into room that is already there.

It went through the same checks as the two changes in 0.6.0. It was first built as a sketch and shown, beside the bar 0.6.0 shipped, to the person whose first-time user had not found either; this is the one they chose.

It was driven by hand in the built app — a copy stamped like the release build, under preferences of its own, against a throwaway disk image, with every removal checked on disk. A box was ticked, and the same spot clicked again without moving the pointer: the mark came off, which is the first mark moving nothing, seen from the outside. The list was opened and a mark taken off from it; a folder moved to the Trash from the bar and brought back with `⌘Z`; a file deleted for good through the confirmation; and nineteen things marked with the list open in a window at its shortest, where the header, the banner, the tabs and the bar, the list, the tree, the map and the status bar were all in sight.

A second model reviewed it. It found one thing, and it was real: the README's words had moved with the bar and the two pictures under them had not. It checked, and found nothing wrong with, the tabs keeping their place across the first mark, the room allowed for the rest of the window while the list is open, and the six numbers on the guide's picture still pointing at what their cards describe.

Then the release was read whole — the difference from 0.6.0, as one piece — by a third model, and that is where the thing that mattered was found. Every check of *the first mark moves nothing* had been made with the list shut. With it open, the list is above the table and was as tall as the marks then standing: each box ticked added a row to it and moved the table down a row, so that a second click in the same place was on the box of the row above. 0.6.0 could not do this, since its list was under the table. The verdict was to fix that first, and it is the fix described above: the list is held at the height it opened with. It was confirmed in the built app — a box ticked with the list open, and the same spot clicked again, took the same mark back off — and reviewed like any other change.

Two smaller things came with it. The check named for the bar being beside the tabs had never asked whether it was, and now does. And closing the Full Disk Access banner slides the bar into the banner's place; that one was left, and is among the limits above.

Last, every feature was clicked through on the bundle the release workflow builds, on a throwaway disk image with every removal checked on disk: the guide on first launch and its keys; a click on a row giving its table the keyboard; the bar, its list, **Move to Trash** and `⌘Z`; Quick Look; a rescan keeping its place; file-type focus; the treemap's zoom and `⌘`-click; search; **Delete…** through its confirmation; the right-click menu; and nineteen marks with the list open in a window at its shortest. One run of it went wrong and was the test's doing, not the app's: reopened, the test copy came back at its smallest size, and clicks worked out for the default size landed on other rows. That pass was made before the fix above; once it was in, the bundle was built again and the part it touches was gone through on that one — a box ticked with the list open and the same spot clicked again, then **Move to Trash**, which took the one file that had been marked to begin with and nothing else.

The self-test grew from 759 checks to 763. The seven that held the bar to the foot of the table were rewritten to hold it to the tabs' row, to the right of the tabs, and to hold the table still: the same height, at the same place, before the first mark, after it, after a second, with the list open and a mark made or taken off, and after the last is cleared.
