# Wizzzee 0.6.0

Wizzzee is a disk space analyzer for macOS in the shape of [WizTree](https://diskanalyzer.com/): a folder tree with sizes, a list of the biggest files, and a colored treemap you can click into. It scans a full startup disk — around 3.9 million files — in roughly 15 seconds.

**This release is about the first five minutes.** Both changes in it come from watching someone use the app for the first time. They ticked three boxes and could not find what to do with them until the corner of the window was pointed out. And most of what the app can do — that `Space` marks a row, that the filter reads `>1gb older:1y`, that `⌘Z` brings back what went to the Trash — was written down in a README they had not read.

## What is marked, and what to do with it, across the foot of the window

Marking was the part of the app that asked the most of someone new. The count of what was marked was a chip in the smallest type in the window, at the bottom left, and **Move to Trash** and **Delete…** were in a list that the chip had to be clicked to open.

From the first mark a bar now runs across the window, above the status bar and on every tab:

- **how many things are marked**, and the space on disk that removing them gives back;
- **Move to Trash**, **Delete…** and **Clear Marks** for the lot, with nothing to open first;
- **Show List**, which opens the list of marked items above the bar and leaves the bar where it was, so the button that opened the list is the one that shuts it.

It arrives lit and fades to a tint, so it is seen arriving by someone whose eyes are on a checkbox at the other end of the window. **Move to Trash** is the one put forward, since it is the one `⌘Z` takes back; it moves without asking, as it did from the list, and **Delete…** still asks.

The list is a row for each mark, with its size, where it is, and a way to take the mark back off. In a short window a long list scrolls instead of taking the room the rest of the window needs.

The status bar under it is set in the size the tables are, a point larger than it was, and is one height whatever is in it: it used to grow a few points whenever **Undo** or **Stop** was showing, and move everything above it.

## A guide, the first time you open it

The first time Wizzzee is opened it now puts up a guide over the window. Seven pages, one for each part of the app:

| Page | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Start with a Scan | choosing a volume or a folder, Scan and Stop, a rescan that keeps your place, Full Disk Access |
| Tree View | opening folders, sorting, Size against On Disk, File Types |
| Treemap | selecting, zooming in and back out along the path, marking with `⌘`-click, hiding the map |
| File View and Search | names, `>1gb`, `older:1y`, `ext:dmg`, `kind:folder`, and the count of what was found |
| Look Before Removing | Quick Look, the right-click menu, double-click, the notes beside a name |
| Mark and Remove | marks, the bar, Move to Trash and Delete…, Undo, the Finder keys, what can't be removed |
| Keyboard Shortcuts | every key the app answers, under five headings |

Each of the first six has a picture of that part of the window, drawn small, with a numbered dot on each thing worth knowing; a map of the window with the part lit, and where it is in words; and a card for every number, saying what can be done there and on which keys.

- **Don't show this again**, at its foot, stops it opening with the app. Until that is ticked it opens once each launch.
- It stays under **Help ▸ Welcome to Wizzzee**, on two buttons in the About tab, and on `⌘/`, which opens it at the shortcuts.
- `Return` and `→` turn the page, `←` turns back, `Esc` shuts it, and the list at the left goes to any page.
- While it is up, the menus' keys are off. The guide names `⌘⌫` and `⌘R` to someone who cannot see the window behind it, and neither does anything until it is shut.

If you are upgrading you will see it once too. Everything in it is news to someone who never had a guide.

## Also fixed

- **A click on a row left the keyboard behind.** In 0.5.0, on current macOS, a click selected a row and the keys then did nothing to it until `Tab` had been pressed: `↑` and `↓` did not move the selection, `→` and `←` did not open or shut a folder, `Space` did not mark, and `Esc` in File Types did not let go of the type. A table that is clicked now has the keyboard, as tables do everywhere else on the Mac. Everything the guide says about those keys depends on it.
- **A row brought into view could slide the table sideways.** In a window at its narrowest the tree's table has less room than its columns take, and scrolls sideways as well as down. A tile clicked on the treemap, or a line clicked in the list of marks, brought the row to the middle of the table both ways: the rows moved left under headers that stayed put, and the checkboxes and the start of every name went off the edge. That was in 0.5.0. The row is still brought half-way down, and the table is left where it was across.

## Keys

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `⌘/` | The guide, at its page of keyboard shortcuts |
| `Return` `→` `←` `Esc` | In the guide: next page, next, back, shut |

## Limits

- The guide opens with the app until **Don't show this again** is ticked. Shutting it is not asking it to stay away.
- **Help ▸ Welcome to Wizzzee has no key.** `⌘?` is the one a Help item is given, and macOS takes it to open the Help menu — where the item is.
- The guide can't be opened while a removal is running: it would cover the status bar, which is where **Stop** is.
- `Return` turns the guide's pages, and in the window behind it `Return` is **Scan**, as it always is. One press too many on the last page starts a scan.
- The guide's pictures are drawings of the window, not photographs of it, and show a disk that is not yours.
- A plain multiple selection — several rows picked with Shift or `⌘`, none of them ticked — still has its two buttons at the top right, under **Size / On Disk**. Only marks have the bar.
- In a window at its shortest, with the treemap showing, the list of marks is about six rows high and scrolls. It takes its full ten from a window about 750 points high.

## Installing

Download `Wizzzee-0.6.0-macos-universal.zip` below, unzip it, and move `Wizzzee.app` to Applications, replacing the old one.

- **First launch.** The app is not notarized, so macOS blocks it the first time. Open it once, dismiss the warning, then go to **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and click **Open Anyway**. macOS asks only once.
- **Full Disk Access has to be granted again.** macOS ties that permission to the exact build, so the grant from an earlier version does not carry over. In **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access**, remove the old Wizzzee entry and add the new app. Without it, Mail, Messages, Safari and other users' folders are skipped and the totals read low.

It runs on macOS 14 or newer, on Apple silicon and Intel.

## Internal

**The bar** is the old list's header moved out from under a click, and the chip gone. The one piece of new machinery is the list's height. It used to be as tall as its rows up to 220 points, with the buttons inside that; with the buttons in a bar of their own and a taller status bar, a long list no longer fitted a window at its shortest. The stack would not share the room out — the tree and the treemap are an AppKit split, which takes the least it will do with whatever it is offered and draws over the tabs above it to get it — so the window's height is measured and the list is given what that height can spare.

**The guide** is a sheet. Its pictures are drawn in SwiftUI from the app's own colours and the treemap's own palette, so there is no image to ship or to keep in step with the app, and they follow light and dark. Whether it opens with the app is one stored value, `showsWelcome`, which `--prefs` reports; `--uishot --welcome N` draws any page of it.

This release is two changes. Each went through the same two checks before it was merged.

Each was driven by hand in the built app — a copy stamped like the release build, under preferences of its own, against a throwaway disk image, with every removal checked on disk — by the model that wrote it, through the real pointer and keyboard. That found what no test had. A row brought into view slid a narrow table sideways, and had since 0.5.0. The status bar changed height when **Undo** appeared. With the guide open, `⌘R` ran a scan and `⌘2` changed the tab behind it. And `⌘?`, the key first given to the guide, opened the Help menu instead. It also overturned a fix: the first cure for a long list of marks in a short window drew correctly off screen and wrongly in the app, where the tree was drawn across the tabs.

A second model reviewed each one, and every finding was checked against the code before anything was changed. On the bar: four findings, all four real — the long list among them — and approved on the second pass. On the guide it was asked above all to read every sentence against what the app does, and most of its eighteen findings were sentences that were not quite true: that a tile clicked on the map always scrolls the tree to its row, that `Esc` empties the filter from anywhere, that a double-click on the map shows a file in Finder. Four more came on later passes. Twenty were acted on. One, rated the most serious — that **Edit ▸ Undo** stays live under the guide — was tried in the built app and does not happen: the item is disabled while the guide is up, and `⌘Z` leaves the Trash alone. One was agreed with and left: the check on the guide's text holds its numbers and its keys to each other, and cannot tell a true sentence from a false one.

The build check caught one more. A new test made its window taller to watch the list of marks take the room, and the build machine's screen is shorter than the window it asked for: AppKit holds a window with a title bar to the height of the screen.

Then the release was read whole — the difference from 0.5.0, as one piece — by a third model, for what the two changes do to each other and to what was already there. Its verdict was to ship: it found no way for the release to remove something that was not asked for, and raised seven small things. Three were acted on. The guide could be opened over a removal that was under way, covering its **Stop**; a check that claimed to measure the window at its fullest did not always; and the README counted four numbers on a picture that has six. Four were left, each with the reason written down; one of them, about `Return`, is among the limits above.

Last, every feature was clicked through once more on a build stamped like the one that ships, and the most useful finding of the release came out of that, about the release before it. A build made on a developer's Mac says it was made against an older SDK than the released one does, and the system treats the two differently. Stamped like the release, a click on a row left the keyboard with the window — and so did 0.5.0, downloaded and run the same way. That is the fix listed first under *Also fixed*. It was then reviewed like any other change.

The same difference had two of the new checks passing on the build machine and failing in the bundle it built. They looked for the bar's buttons among AppKit's, and in the released build SwiftUI draws its buttons without any. The checks now ask the views where they have been put, and were run under both stamps before this was tagged.

The self-test grew from 714 checks to 759. The new ones put the real window together off screen and ask it what it is showing: that the first mark puts four buttons under the table, that a long list of marks in the shortest window leaves every button inside it, that a row revealed in a table too narrow for its columns leaves the table where it was across, that a click on a row gives its table the keyboard, and that the guide comes up as a sheet when asked for and at no other time.
